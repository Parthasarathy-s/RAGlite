A large language model (LLM) is a neural network trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next token. A token is a small piece of text, roughly three quarters of a word. The model reads a prompt and generates a reply one token at a time. Every model has a context window, which is the maximum number of tokens it can read and write in one request.

LLMs can make things up, which is called hallucination. They also do not know about your private documents or events after their training cutoff. Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, fixes this by looking up relevant text from your own documents and placing it in the prompt, so the model answers from facts you supplied.

An embedding is a list of numbers that represents the meaning of a piece of text. Texts with similar meaning get similar embeddings. Cosine similarity measures how close two embeddings are. A vector database stores embeddings and quickly finds the nearest ones to a query.
